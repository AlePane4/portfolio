Retail Ops · Store Performance

A composite scoring system to rank stores across a grocery network

For a large grocery chain, store performance was judged almost only on sales, ignoring customer satisfaction and operational quality. I designed a scoring framework across three KPI areas, where each KPI is converted into points via thresholds and the total score produces a transparent network-wide ranking.

Scope
180+ stores · monthly refresh
Methods
KPI design · threshold scoring · composite ranking
Stack
SQL · Python · Power BI

The framework: three KPI areas

The core idea: a great store is not just a store that sells. The score balances what customers feel, what the store earns, and how well the team runs it. Weights were agreed with operations leadership.

Weight 30%

Customer satisfaction

  • NPS (post-visit surveys)
  • Google reviews rating
  • Complaint rate per 1k tickets
  • Mystery shopper score

Weight 40%

Economic performance

  • Sales vs target
  • Sales growth YoY (like-for-like)
  • Sales by department vs plan
  • Shrinkage & waste rate

Weight 30%

Know-how & operations

  • Product knowledge assessment
  • Internal audit score
  • Inspection / compliance checks
  • Planogram & availability checks

From KPI to points: threshold example

Each KPI maps to 0–3 points through thresholds, so different units (an NPS, a %, an audit grade) become comparable. Example for two KPIs:

KPI 3 points 2 points 1 point 0 points
NPS (CSAT area) ≥ 60 45 – 59 30 – 44 < 30
Google reviews (CSAT area) ≥ 4.4 4.0 – 4.3 3.5 – 3.9 < 3.5
Sales vs target (Economic area) ≥ 103% 98 – 102% 93 – 97% < 93%
Internal audit (Know-how area) ≥ 90/100 80 – 89 70 – 79 < 70

Area score = sum of KPI points, normalised to 100. Final score = weighted sum of the three areas (30 / 40 / 30).

Impact

The ranking changed the conversation: several "top sellers" turned out to be weak on CSAT and know-how, while smaller stores with excellent operations became visible for the first time.
Bottom-quartile stores get a structured action plan per weak area (e.g. low know-how → targeted training), and their score trajectory is tracked month over month.
Because scoring is threshold-based and transparent, store managers can see exactly which KPI to move to gain points: the framework became a self-service improvement tool, not just a report.
