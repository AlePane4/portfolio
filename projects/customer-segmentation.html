Customer · Segmentation & CRM

From transactions to actionable clusters and a marketing playbook

One-size-fits-all CRM campaigns were losing effectiveness. Starting from raw transaction data, I built a behavioral segmentation that groups customers into five clusters the marketing team can actually name, understand and target, each with its own strategy.

Data
24 months of transactions · loyalty card base
Methods
Extended RFM features · K-Means · silhouette validation
Stack
Python · scikit-learn · SQL · Tableau

Approach

The key choice was the feature set: beyond classic RFM (recency, frequency, monetary), I added behavioral dimensions (basket composition, category breadth, promo sensitivity, preferred day/time), so clusters describe how people shop, not just how much.

Feature engineering RFM + basket size, category breadth, promo share, visit patterns per customer.
Preprocessing Outlier treatment, log transforms, standardisation.
Clustering K-Means; k selected via silhouette score and business interpretability.
Profiling Each cluster described with stats, personas and value contribution.
Activation Cluster labels pushed to the CRM weekly; strategies co-designed with marketing.

Output 1: The five clusters

Example output: each segment with its share of the base, key stats, and the marketing strategy attached to it. Names were chosen with the marketing team: a cluster nobody can name is a cluster nobody will use.

Ambassadors

8% of base · 31% of revenue

Very frequent, high-spend, wide category breadth, low promo dependency. The brand's true fans.

€142avg monthly spend
6.2visits / month
12%promo share

Strategy: reward & amplify VIP tier with exclusive previews and services (not discounts, they don't need them). Referral program: turn advocacy into acquisition. Protect at all costs: churn alerts with priority follow-up.

Loyal routiners

22% of base · 34% of revenue

Regular weekly shop, stable basket, mid spend, habit-driven. The backbone of the business.

€89avg monthly spend
4.1visits / month
21%promo share

Strategy: grow the basket Cross-sell into categories they don't buy yet (personalised recommendations). Missions-based offers ("complete your weekly shop"). Avoid over-discounting what they already buy at full price.

Deal seekers

18% of base · 15% of revenue

Shop mainly on promotion, cherry-pick offers, spike during campaigns, quiet otherwise.

€54avg monthly spend
2.8visits / month
58%promo share

Strategy: convert to habit Personalised offers on non-promo categories, gamified challenges (streaks, points multipliers) to increase frequency. Measure incrementality carefully: avoid subsidising purchases that would happen anyway.

Occasional shoppers

37% of base · 14% of revenue

Infrequent visits, small baskets, narrow category range. Convenience-driven, low engagement.

€28avg monthly spend
1.2visits / month
33%promo share

Strategy: activate a second visit Welcome-back journeys, time-boxed "come back within 14 days" incentives, app onboarding push. Success metric: share moving to ≥2 visits/month, not campaign redemption alone.

At-risk / fading

15% of base · 6% of revenue

Were regulars, now declining recency and frequency. Early churn signals visible in the data.

€19avg monthly spend
0.6visits / month
−41%spend vs 6m ago

Strategy: win back, then learn Two-step win-back flow: strong personalised offer, then a "why did you leave?" micro-survey for non-responders. Feedback loops into the Voice of Customer program. Cap investment: not every customer is worth saving.

Output 2: Value concentration

The classic message that lands with executives: 30% of customers (Ambassadors + Loyal routiners) generate 65% of revenue. Retention on these two clusters is worth more than acquisition anywhere else.

Impact

The marketing team moved from one generic weekly newsletter to five differentiated journeys, one per cluster, with content and incentives designed around each segment's behavior.
Cluster membership became a shared language across teams: campaign briefs, media plans and store communications all reference the same five segments.
Segments are refreshed weekly and monitored for migration: the share of customers moving "up" (e.g. Occasional → Loyal) is now a standing KPI of CRM effectiveness.
